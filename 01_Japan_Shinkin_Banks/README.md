# Japan Shinkin Banks — Interest Rate Risk Project

## Research Question

**Which Japanese shinkin banks are structurally vulnerable to rising interest rates?**

This project investigates how the return of positive interest rates in Japan affects the balance sheets of shinkin banks (信用金庫).

The objective is not simply to identify institutions reporting large unrealized losses.

The objective is to understand the financial structure behind those losses.

In particular, the project examines the relationship between:

- Deposits
- Loans
- Loan-to-deposit ratios
- Securities holdings
- Japanese government bond holdings
- Unrealized gains and losses on securities
- Capital
- Interest income
- Funding costs
- Profitability

## Starting Observation

For many years, some shinkin banks accumulated deposits faster than they could expand lending in their local economies.

The excess funds had to go somewhere.

A significant portion was invested in securities.

During Japan's prolonged low-interest-rate environment, this structure could remain relatively stable.

When interest rates rise, however, the market value of existing low-yield bonds falls.

This creates a new question:

**What happens to financial institutions whose balance sheets were built during decades of near-zero interest rates?**

## Research Approach

The project will compare financial data across Japanese shinkin banks.

Rather than looking at a single indicator, the analysis will examine the interaction between several balance-sheet variables.

A simplified capital flow can be expressed as:

**Deposits → Loans → Excess Funds → Securities → Interest Rate Exposure**

The working assumption is that vulnerability cannot be determined by unrealized bond losses alone.

For example, two institutions may hold similar amounts of securities but have very different abilities to absorb losses depending on:

- Capital strength
- Loan profitability
- Deposit structure
- Securities duration
- Funding costs
- Operating profitability

The purpose of this project is therefore to identify **balance-sheet structures that become vulnerable when interest rates rise.**

## Initial Research Scope

The first stage will focus on publicly available financial disclosures from Japanese shinkin banks.

The initial dataset will attempt to collect:

- Total assets
- Deposits
- Loans
- Securities
- Government bonds
- Local government bonds
- Corporate bonds
- Stocks
- Other securities
- Unrealized gains/losses on securities
- Capital
- Capital adequacy ratio
- Net income
- Interest income
- Interest expenses
- Loan-to-deposit ratio

Additional indicators may be added as the research develops.

## Output

The project aims to build a comparable dataset covering Japanese shinkin banks and to identify patterns of interest-rate vulnerability.

Possible outputs include:

- Comparative balance-sheet analysis
- Interest-rate vulnerability indicators
- Individual shinkin bank case studies
- Regional comparisons
- Historical changes in securities exposure
- A Deep Money research report

The project will evolve as new data, evidence, and observations accumulate.
